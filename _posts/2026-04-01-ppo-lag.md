## Background

Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is one of the most widely used policy gradient algorithms, but standard PPO has no mechanism to enforce safety constraints during training. **PPO-Lag** (PPO with Lagrangian relaxation) addresses this by framing the Constrained MDP (CMDP) as a saddle-point problem, adding a dual variable (the Lagrange multiplier) that penalizes constraint violations.

## The Constrained MDP Formulation

In a CMDP, the objective is to maximize expected return while keeping a cost signal below a threshold $d$:

$$\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_\pi[R] \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbb{E}_\pi[C] \leq d$$

The Lagrangian relaxation converts this to an unconstrained problem:

$$\mathcal{L}(\pi, \lambda) = \mathbb{E}_\pi[R] - \lambda \left(\mathbb{E}_\pi[C] - d\right)$$

The outer loop optimizes $\lambda$ to push constraint satisfaction, while the inner loop optimizes the policy.

## Key Implementation Details

**Dual variable update** — After each epoch, the multiplier is updated via gradient ascent on the Lagrangian:

```python
lambda_lr = 0.01
lambda_val = max(0, lambda_val + lambda_lr * (mean_ep_cost - cost_limit))
```

**Combined loss** — The policy gradient loss merges reward and cost advantages weighted by $\lambda$:

```python
combined_adv = reward_adv - lambda_val * cost_adv
ratio = torch.exp(log_prob - old_log_prob)
surr1 = ratio * combined_adv
surr2 = torch.clamp(ratio, 1 - clip_eps, 1 + clip_eps) * combined_adv
policy_loss = -torch.min(surr1, surr2).mean()
```

## Takeaways

- The dual variable acts as an adaptive penalty: when costs exceed the limit, $\lambda$ grows, making the agent more conservative.
- Separate value heads for reward and cost are necessary — a single critic conflates the two signals.
- Tuning `lambda_lr` relative to the policy learning rate is critical; too large and the policy oscillates, too small and constraints are violated for many epochs before correcting.
